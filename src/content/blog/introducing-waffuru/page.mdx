You should be able to run intelligence on the machine in front of you, and keep what it learns about you right there. Today we are announcing Waffuru, the company building the stack to make that real, and shipping our first product with it: [Pantry](/products/pantry), a **free**, open-source vulnerability scanner for macOS and Linux.

Our mission fits on one line: **Democratize access to intelligence.**

The name is the Japanese word for waffles, ワッフル (waffuru). It is a nod to our respect for Japanese culture and its deep history, a shared love of waffles on the team, and to a mission that does not stop at any border.

## What we _believe_

We wrote our theses down to frame how we’re thinking about artificial intelligence. The short version:

- **Demand for intelligence is limitless. The resources behind it are not.** Power and chips are the ceiling, so the win is more useful work from every unit of compute and energy. Every gain gets spent on more intelligence, so that work never finishes.
- **Good-enough models on hardware you own are here.** Models running on the laptops, desktops and servers people already have are good enough for meaningful work today.
- **Everyone will have a personal assistant, or a team of them.** People want agency, so an assistant has to be a trusted extension of you: helpful, approachable, never scary, and yours.
- **Access must not depend on geography, wealth or credentials.** Raising earning potential everywhere takes open, local access to intelligence.

Two constraints follow: minimize token usage and cost per token, on consumer and data-centre hardware alike, and keep your data on your hardware unless you choose otherwise. [Our about page](/about#beliefs) has all seven theses and the values behind them.

## The _stack_

To run capable models and agents on hardware you own or rent, every layer between your question and the silicon has to be lean, fast and yours. So we are building all of them.

Six products, from the top down:

- **Pantry** keeps every machine safe: it finds what is exposed and patches it. It is **free** and open source today.
- **Waffle** is the agent harness, with W, our assistant, inside. It runs local models first and reaches for the cloud only when a task needs more. Coming soon.
- **Whisk** mixes the right documents and context into every request, from your own notes and code, with citations.
- **Syrup** is the control plane: monitoring, telemetry, access control and cost, for every GPU and every engine on it.
- **Butter** is the multi-modal inference engine: text, vision and audio, streamed, in Swift for macOS and iOS and Rust for everything else, with minimal dependencies.
- **Iron** is the kernels: written once in Rust and optimized for each chip. Metal first, then CUDA, with ROCm and Vulkan running and not tuned yet.

Your request goes down through them and the answer comes back the same way, on hardware you own or rent, and nothing leaves it unless you say so. Each product works on its own, and together they do more.

## First out the _kitchen_: Pantry

Before AI runs on your machines, they have to be safe. Pantry is the tool we built to keep ours that way, and today it is yours, **free**.

Security teams ask two questions: what am I exposed to, and how fast can I patch it? CVE lists and feeds answer the first with noise, and the tools that answer it well are mostly behind a paywall. Pantry closes the gap. It checks everything installed on your machine, and every repo on it, against open vulnerability data, reports only what applies to your exact versions, and fixes what it safely can.

- **Rapid.** Answers are cached in SQLite on your machine, with an audit trail of every run, so a second scan the same day takes seconds.
- **No AI, no telemetry.** Plain, deterministic code. Only package names and versions leave your machine, to look them up.
- **Written in Rust** for speed and safety, dependency-light, and every release is versioned, hashed and verifiable before it runs.
- **For people and agents**, on a personal computer or a fleet of servers.
- **macOS and Linux today.** Windows support is planned, and if we see enough demand, we’ll bump up the timeline.

Read [how Pantry works](/blog/introducing-pantry), see the [Pantry page](/products/pantry), or [read the source on GitHub](https://github.com/waffuruai/pantry).
