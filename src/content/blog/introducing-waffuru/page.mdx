You should be able to run intelligence on the machine in front of you, and keep what it learns about you right there. Today we are announcing Waffuru, the company building the stack to make that real, and shipping our first product with it: [Pantry](/products/pantry), a free, open-source vulnerability scanner for macOS and Linux.

## What we _believe_

We wrote our theses down so you can hold us to them. The short version:

- **Demand for intelligence is limitless. The resources behind it are not.** Power and chips are the ceiling, so the win is more useful work from every unit of compute and energy. Every gain gets spent on more intelligence, so that work never finishes.
- **Good-enough models on hardware you own are close.** Within about two years, models running on the laptops, desktops and servers people already have will be good enough for meaningful work.
- **Everyone will have a personal assistant, or a team of them.** People want agency, so an assistant has to be a trusted extension of you: helpful, approachable, never scary, and yours.
- **Access must not depend on geography, wealth or credentials.** Raising earning potential everywhere takes open, local access to intelligence.

Two constraints follow. What we build has to run on consumer hardware, and your data stays on your machine unless you choose otherwise. The [about page](/about#beliefs) has all seven theses and the values behind them.

## The _stack_

To put a capable assistant on hardware you already own, every layer between your question and the silicon has to be lean, fast and yours. So we are building all of them.

<StackFlow copy={home.en.stack.flow} className="not-prose my-10" />

Your request comes in through a **control plane** (Syrup) that shows every GPU and every engine running on it. **Memory** (Whisk) brings in context from your own notes and code, with citations. The **engine** (Butter) runs the model, with no Python in the serving path. The **kernels** (Iron) do the math, written once and tuned for each chip: Metal first, then CUDA, with ROCm and Vulkan running and not tuned yet. The answer comes back the same way, and nothing leaves your hardware unless you say so.

The stack is private and in early access while we harden it. The [home page](/) shows how the pieces fit, and you can [request a seat](https://forms.gle/SHaVSRRWmZKy8UJy7).

## First off the _griddle_: Pantry

Before we put an assistant on your machine, the machine has to be safe. Pantry is the tool we built to keep ours that way, and today it is yours, free.

Security teams ask two questions: what am I exposed to, and how fast can I patch it? CVE lists and feeds answer the first with noise, and the tools that answer it well are mostly behind a paywall. Pantry closes the gap. It checks everything installed on your machine, and every repo on it, against open vulnerability data, reports only what applies to your exact versions, and fixes what it safely can.

- **Rapid.** Answers are cached in SQLite on your machine, with an audit trail of every run, so a second scan the same day takes seconds.
- **No AI, no telemetry.** Plain, deterministic code. Only package names and versions leave your machine, to look them up.
- **Written in Rust** for speed and safety, dependency-light, and every release is versioned, hashed and verifiable before it runs.
- **For people and agents**, on a personal computer or a fleet of servers.
- **macOS and Linux today.** Windows support is planned, and demand sets the date.

Read [how Pantry works](/blog/introducing-pantry), see the [Pantry page](/products/pantry), or [read the source on GitHub](https://github.com/waffuruai/wscanner).
