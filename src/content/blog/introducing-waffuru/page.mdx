You should be able to run intelligence on the machine in front of you, and keep what it learns about you right there. That is the job we set out to do, and our mission fits on one line: **Democratize access to intelligence.**

The name is the Japanese word for waffles, ワッフル (waffuru). It nods to Tom’s love of manga, to a shared love of waffles on the team, and to a mission that does not stop at any border.

## Why _now_

Demand for intelligence is limitless. The resources that power it are not. Scaling inference takes more than new datacenters and more power; it takes more useful work from every unit of compute and energy. The next generation of infrastructure has to grow access to intelligence faster than it grows cost, energy demand and environmental impact.

When power is the ceiling, efficiency is the variable you can still move. Tokens per watt and dollars per workload are what we get to maximize. Efficiency will not shrink demand, either. Every gain gets spent on more intelligence, the old Jevons paradox, so the appetite for compute only grows.

The other end of the curve is moving too. We think that within about two years, models running on hardware people already own will be good enough for meaningful work, because Moore’s law and fast AI progress keep compounding.

We expect everyone to have a personal assistant, or a team of them, talking to each other. People want agency, so an assistant has to be a trusted extension of yourself: helpful and approachable, not scary. That relationship will be as personal as the one you have with your phone. It should not depend on where you live, what you earn or which credentials you hold, and raising earning potential everywhere takes open, local access to intelligence.

## What we are _building_

Four products, numbered from the metal up. Iron compiles the kernels. Butter runs models on them. Waffle watches the GPUs. Syrup remembers what you were working on. Each works alone, and together they are the foundation for local-first personal assistants.

### Iron, the kernels

[Iron](/products/iron) is one kernel definition, four backends. You write a kernel in a Rust-embedded DSL. It lowers to a shared IR, then to Metal, CUDA, HIP/ROCm or Vulkan SPIR-V, and a versioned C and Rust ABI lets the hosts you already have call it. Today that is about 1,139 kernel declarations, and our HIP and Vulkan test corpus passes 4,164 of 4,164 cases bit-accurate. This is the whole definition of an `exp` kernel:

```rust title="unary.rs"
#[kernel(bench(op = "unary", subop = "exp", tol = 1e-4))]
pub fn iron_exp<T>(a: Tensor<T>, out: Tensor<T>) {
    let idx = program_id(0);
    store(out[idx], exp(load(a[idx])));
}
```

You write that. Iron writes the rest.

### Butter, the engine

[Butter](/products/butter) is model serving without Python: Swift on Metal for Apple silicon, Rust for CUDA and the rest. It loads models straight from Hugging Face (safetensors, MLX, JANG and GGUF) with no conversion, and runs 3- to 8-bit quantized checkpoints, hybrid and mixture-of-experts models and a quantized KV cache. `butter serve` speaks the OpenAI API, with streaming, tool calling, speculative decoding and LoRA. Text, vision, speech-to-text, text-to-speech and omni models all run. No Python, no JIT.

### Waffle, the control plane

[Waffle](/products/waffle) shows you every GPU and every engine running on it, from one box under a desk to a neocloud renting out a fleet. Each host runs a small Rust sidecar that reports signed telemetry. It works with Butter, vLLM, SGLang, llama.cpp and other OpenAI-compatible servers, so there is no lock-in.

### Syrup, the memory

[Syrup](/products/syrup) is how your AI remembers what you were working on. It runs local retrieval over your notes and code, compresses embeddings with TurboQuant+, searches lexically and by vector, and returns results with citations over MCP.

## Where we _are_

Early. Our products are private and closed source for now, and access is by request. Here is the hardware picture, stated plainly:

- **Apple silicon, Metal: primary.** Every kernel is written and tuned here first.
- **NVIDIA, CUDA: secondary.** In production on RTX and DGX Spark, with datacenter GPUs in progress.
- **AMD ROCm and Vulkan: runs.** Both work today and neither is tuned yet.

In practice that means MacBooks and Mac Studios, RTX workstations, DGX Spark, Strix Halo and neocloud fleets, with enterprise datacenter GPUs still to come. We are not quoting speed numbers yet.
